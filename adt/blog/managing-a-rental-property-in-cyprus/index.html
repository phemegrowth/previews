Managing a rental property in Cyprus: what owners need to know

Guides · Nov 19, 2026 · 3 min read

Renting out a property in Cyprus can be a steady source of income, whether it is a city apartment in Limassol, a family house in Larnaca or a holiday home in Paphos. But a rental is a small business, and it works best when it is run like one. Here is what every owner should know before, during and after a tenancy.

Before you rent

Get the property ready. Tenants judge a property in the first few minutes. Fix what is broken, service the air conditioning, deep clean, and make sure the basics (hot water, appliances, locks, lighting) all work. A well-presented property rents faster and attracts better tenants.

Set the right rent. Look at comparable properties in the same area, not asking prices from a year ago. Overpricing by a small amount can leave the property empty for months, which usually costs more than the difference.

Decide long-term or short-term. Long-term rentals give stable income and less day-to-day work. Short-term holiday rentals can earn more in season but involve far more management, cleaning and guest contact, and come with their own registration and licensing requirements. Check what applies to your property before you decide.

Choosing a tenant

Take your time. Check identity, income or employment, and references where possible. Be clear from the start about the rent, deposit, duration and what the tenant is responsible for. A careful choice here prevents most of the problems landlords face later.

The paperwork

  • Tenancy agreement: a written agreement covering rent, deposit, duration, renewals, bills, repairs and notice. Involve a lawyer if you are unsure about any of the legal terms.
  • Check-in report: photos, inventory, meter readings and keys, confirmed by both sides.
  • Utilities: agree who holds the electricity and water accounts and who pays common expenses, and put it in writing.
  • Insurance: make sure your building and contents cover reflects that the property is rented.
  • Tax: rental income has tax obligations. Speak to an accountant about what applies to you, especially if you are not tax resident in Cyprus.

During the tenancy

Respond to repair requests quickly. Tenants who feel looked after pay on time, take better care of the property and stay longer. Keep a record of every request and repair, track rent and costs monthly, and inspect the property from time to time, with proper notice to the tenant.

Plan seasonal maintenance too: air conditioning before summer, drains and waterproofing before the winter rains. It is far easier to do this between tenancies or on a planned date than as an emergency.

At the end of the tenancy

Do a check-out against the check-in report, take meter readings, collect keys and agree any deposit deductions based on evidence. Then use the gap before the next tenant to refresh the property and fix anything that has worn out.

If you don't live nearby

Many owners in Cyprus live in another city or another country. Managing a rental from a distance is possible, but only with someone reliable on the ground and clear reporting on what is happening. Without that, small problems go unnoticed until they are big ones.
