Five expensive mistakes landlords make in Cyprus

Guides · Nov 19, 2026 · 2 min read

Most landlord problems in Cyprus don't start with a bad tenant or a broken boiler. They start with a small decision made months earlier: a rushed tenant choice, a missing photo, a repair left for later. Here are five mistakes we see again and again, and how to avoid them.

1. Choosing a tenant too quickly

An empty apartment feels expensive, so it is tempting to accept the first person who wants it. But a month of vacancy costs far less than a tenant who stops paying, damages the property or is difficult to move on.

How to avoid it: check identity, income or employment and, where possible, references from a previous landlord. Meet the person. If something doesn't add up, wait for the next applicant.

2. Relying on a handshake

Informal arrangements work until they don't. Without a clear written agreement, disputes about rent increases, repairs, notice periods or the deposit become one person's word against another's.

How to avoid it: use a proper written tenancy agreement that covers rent, deposit, duration, who pays which bills and how repairs are handled. If you are unsure about the legal side, involve a lawyer before signing, not after a problem appears.

3. No record of the property's condition

At the end of a tenancy, the question is always the same: was this damage already there? Without dated photos and an inventory from the day the tenant moved in, deposit discussions become guesswork.

How to avoid it: do a documented check-in and check-out. Photograph every room, record meter readings, list furniture and appliances, and have both sides confirm it.

4. Waiting for things to break

Cyprus is hard on buildings. Long hot summers push air conditioning units to their limit, humidity near the coast encourages damp and corrosion, and winter rain finds every weak seal on a roof or balcony. Skipping routine servicing saves a little now and costs a lot later, often in the middle of a tenancy, when the tenant is least patient.

How to avoid it: plan maintenance by season. Service air conditioning before summer, check roofs, drains and waterproofing before the rains, and fix small leaks as soon as they are reported. We wrote more about this in why preventive maintenance protects property value.

5. Managing from a distance without a system

Many owners live abroad or are simply busy. Managing a property through occasional phone calls and favours from friends works for a while, but problems get noticed late, receipts get lost and nobody has the full picture.

How to avoid it: put one accountable person or company in charge, with clear reporting on rent, costs, repairs and inspections. You should be able to see what is happening with your property at any time, without having to ask.

The common thread

None of these mistakes is dramatic on the day it happens. They are small shortcuts that become expensive over time. A good routine, good records and the right people around you prevent most of them.
