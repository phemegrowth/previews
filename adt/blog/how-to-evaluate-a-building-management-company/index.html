How to evaluate a building management company

Guides · Nov 19, 2026 · 3 min read

A residential building is a shared investment. When it is well managed, nobody notices: the lift works, the entrance is clean, the common expenses make sense and problems are fixed before they become arguments. When it is badly managed, everyone notices, and the value of every apartment suffers.

If your building committee or owners' association is choosing a management company, or reconsidering the current one, here are the questions worth asking.

1. What exactly will they manage?

Ask for the scope in writing. A complete building management service usually covers cleaning of common areas, maintenance of shared systems (lifts, pumps, lighting, gates, gardens, pools), collection of common expenses, supplier management, insurance follow-up and support for owners' meetings. If something you expect is not on the list, ask whether it is included or charged separately.

2. How do they handle money?

This is the question that matters most to owners. A professional company should be able to explain:

  • How common expenses are calculated and collected.
  • How the building's money is kept separate and accounted for.
  • How often owners receive a financial statement, and what it shows.
  • How unpaid contributions are followed up, fairly and consistently.
  • How larger expenses are approved before they are committed.

If the answers are vague, treat that as a warning.

3. Who are their suppliers and technicians?

Ask who services the lift, who does the cleaning and who responds to plumbing or electrical faults. Do they compare quotes for larger works? Do they check that licensed work is done by properly licensed professionals? A good manager is only as reliable as the people they send.

4. How do they plan maintenance?

Look for a company that works from a maintenance plan, not a list of complaints. They should know when each shared system was last serviced, what is due next, and which larger works (roof waterproofing, painting, pump replacement) the building should start budgeting for.

5. How fast do they respond, and how will you know?

Ask how residents report a problem, how quickly it is acknowledged, and how owners can see that it was fixed. Emergencies such as a stuck lift, a burst pipe or a power failure in common areas need a clear process, at any hour.

6. How do they communicate?

Buildings in Cyprus often have owners from several countries, some living in the building and some abroad. The company should communicate clearly, regularly and in the languages your owners speak, and should be able to help run owners' meetings and record decisions properly.

7. Can they show you their reporting?

Ask to see an example of the reports they send to buildings they already manage. Clear, regular reporting on finances, maintenance and open issues is the simplest way to tell a professional company from an informal one.

8. What happens if you want to change?

Finally, read the contract terms: duration, notice period and how records, keys and funds are handed over at the end. A confident company does not need to lock you in.

A simple test

Ask every candidate the same questions and compare the answers side by side, not just the fees. The cheapest offer often leaves out the things that keep a building in good condition.
