What does professional property management in Cyprus actually include?

Guides · Nov 19, 2026 · 3 min read

“Property management” can mean almost anything in Cyprus. For one company it means collecting the rent. For another it means a phone number you call when something breaks. Before you hand over your apartment, villa or portfolio, it helps to know what a professional service should actually cover, so you can compare like with like.

Here is what we believe a complete property management service in Cyprus should include, whether you live on the island or abroad.

1. A clear starting point

Good management starts with an inspection, not an invoice. Before taking over a property, the manager should walk through it, record its condition with photos, check the main systems (air conditioning, water heater, electrics, plumbing, appliances) and agree with you what needs attention first.

That first report becomes the reference point for everything that follows: tenant check-ins, deposit discussions and maintenance planning.

2. Finding and checking the right tenants

If the property is to be rented, the manager should help you decide on the right rent level for the area, present the property properly and handle viewings. More importantly, they should check prospective tenants before anyone signs: identity, employment or income, and references where possible.

A cheap tenant search that ends in unpaid rent or a damaged apartment is the most expensive mistake an owner can make.

3. Tenancy agreements and handovers

A written tenancy agreement protects both sides. Your manager should coordinate it with you and, where needed, with your lawyer, then run a proper check-in: meter readings, an inventory, photos and keys signed for. The same goes at check-out, so that any deposit deductions are based on evidence, not memory.

4. Rent and running costs

Collecting rent on time, following up on late payments and keeping track of the recurring costs that come with owning property in Cyprus: common expenses for the building, utility accounts, municipal charges and insurance renewals. You should always know what came in, what went out and why.

5. Maintenance, repairs and emergencies

This is where most of the real work sits. A professional manager:

  • Responds to tenant requests quickly and keeps a record of each one.
  • Has trusted local technicians for plumbing, electrics, air conditioning and general repairs.
  • Asks for your approval before larger costs, with quotes where it makes sense.
  • Plans preventive maintenance, such as air conditioning servicing before summer, instead of waiting for things to fail.
  • Knows what to do in an emergency, at any time of year.

6. Regular inspections

Even with a great tenant, someone should look at the property regularly. Small leaks, damp patches and worn seals are cheap to fix when they are found early and expensive when they are found late. For empty or holiday homes, regular visits matter even more.

7. Reporting you can actually read

You should not have to chase your manager for updates. Clear, regular reporting, with photos, costs and what is planned next, is what lets you own property in Cyprus without worrying about it. At ADT, regular reporting is part of every management agreement for exactly this reason.

8. One point of contact

Finally, a good manager is a person, not a call centre. You should know who looks after your property, be able to reach them easily, and get answers in your own language.

What to ask before you sign

When you compare companies, ask each one the same questions: What exactly is included in the monthly fee? What costs extra? How do you choose and check tenants? Who are your technicians? How often will I hear from you, and in what format? The answers will tell you a lot more than the price.
